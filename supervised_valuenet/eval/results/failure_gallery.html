failure gallery — the backward planner's structural ceiling

The puzzles no subgoal plan can say

The backward planner writes its solutions in a small vocabulary: “park helper H on support cell S so the slider stops on bottleneck cell B.” An exhaustive, network-free probe proved that for 42 of 450 benchmark puzzles at the base scale (9.3%) and 71 of 450 at 6 robots (15.8%) no playable plan exists in that vocabulary at all — capping the backward planner at 90.7% and 84.2% respectively, no matter how well its networks are trained. This page shows what those puzzles actually need: the forward (move-by-move) planner solved 75 of the 113 for real, and each family below steps through one such solution up to the exact moment the subgoal language runs out of words.

Where the verdicts and the solutions come from

Two independent measurements meet here.

  • The impossibility verdicts come from the ceiling probe: the hand-coded subgoal search run exhaustively (no neural network, so guidance is not the bottleneck), which either finds a strictly-playable plan or proves none exists. Verdicts split into NO_COMPLETE_PLAN (no complete subgoal plan can even be built: 22 base / 48 six-robot) and NO_REALIZABLE_PLAN (plans exist but none survives legal playback: 20 / 23). The verdicts are cap-independent: re-probed at 4× the plan-width cap, 22 of 22 base and 48 of 48 six-robot NO_COMPLETE_PLAN instances stayed structural failures. Probed failures that are NOT structural are excluded from this page: 7 base and 28 six-robot puzzles have a playable plan the networks missed (guidance headroom, not a language limit), and 6 six-robot probes hit their time cap without a verdict.
  • The solutions come from the forward move planner — a pure network + search system (no oracle) — run on exactly these puzzles at its standard operating point (top-5 proposals, 1200-expansion budget). Every move sequence shown replays legally, move by move, under the ground-truth slide simulator; the replay is re-checked when this page is built.

The subgoal vocabulary has four hard rules, and each failure family below is a real maneuver that breaks one of them:

  • Rule 1 — supports need walls. A helper may only be parked on a cell where a wall can hold it (a cell reachable by a walls-only slide).
  • Rule 2 — supports are forever. Once parked, a helper never moves again.
  • Rule 3 — one robot, one job. Each robot fills at most one plan role; the target robot always fills the “reach the goal” role.
  • Rule 4 — blockers are assumed to dissolve. Route costs are computed as if robots in the way slide off for free; the vocabulary has no actual “step aside” move.
One honest caveat. The impossibility verdicts are solution-independent (exhaustive proof). The family assignment is not: it is read off the one solution the forward planner found, so it names the missing word that solution needed. Different solutions of the same puzzle could stress different missing words; the families are evidence about what the vocabulary lacks, not unique labels per puzzle.

The winning line needs a robot to act as a stopper on a cell that has no adjacent wall. Such a robot can only hold that cell because something else stops it there — another robot, placed first. The subgoal vocabulary admits only wall-holdable cells as parking spots (a support must survive on its own), so no plan can even be written down. This is the mechanism of the worked example in the B1 design note: pass through the cell first, then park a robot behind — the cell is empty at one moment and occupied later.

Every individual maneuver in the winning line is nameable: all stoppers stand on wall-holdable cells, nobody vacates, nobody serves twice, and no paper walk short-circuits the search. The obstruction sits one level deeper, in how plans are assembled: a claimed route may only name a stopper that is already part of the plan (the declared-stopper invariant), and the assembly order this puzzle needs cannot be reached. The exhaustive probe still proves no expressible plan plays out; this is reported as a residual rather than forced under a headline it does not match.
