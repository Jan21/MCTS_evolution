# Failure families — what the structural ceiling is made of

Written 2026-07-17. This document names the maneuvers that the measured
structural failures of the backward (subgoal) planner actually need — the
puzzles for which the ceiling probe proved **no strictly-playable subgoal plan
exists at all** under the old vocabulary: 42 of 450 at the base scale
(16×16, 4 robots; ceiling 90.7%) and 71 of 450 at 6 robots (ceiling ~84.2%).
Companion page: `eval/results/failure_gallery.html` (generated by
`analysis/failure_gallery/build_gallery.py`), which steps through one real
solution per family. All numbers below are reproduced by
`analysis/failure_gallery/diagnose.py` from the pinned artifacts listed at the
end; none are hand-typed estimates.

## Method, in one paragraph

Each structural instance was solved for real by the **forward move planner**
(pure network + search, no oracle) at its standard operating point (top-5,
1200 expansions): **42/42 at base scale**, **33/71 at 6 robots** (all 21
oracle-graded instances plus 12 of the 50 beyond-oracle ones; the 38
unsolved are all beyond-oracle and get no mechanism claim). Every returned
move sequence replays legally under the ground-truth slide simulator
(`simulate.slide`); the replay is re-checked at every build. A trace analyzer then reads, move by move, what stopped each
slide and which robots did work that the subgoal vocabulary has no words for
("gates"). An instance's **family** is its hardest gate — uncovered-by-B1
gates take precedence over park-covered ones, which take precedence over
Layer-1-covered ones — so the family is the one that decides whether the
planned vocabulary extension covers the instance.

**Honest caveat.** The impossibility verdicts are solution-independent
(exhaustive probe). The family labels are not: they are read off the one
solution the forward planner found, so they name the missing words *that
solution* needed. A different solution could stress different words. Two
families are additionally cross-validated against probe internals, noted
below.

## The four rules the families break

The subgoal vocabulary ("park helper H on support cell S so the slider stops
on bottleneck cell B") has four hard rules:

1. **Supports need walls.** Only wall-holdable cells (reachable by a
   walls-only slide) may be named as parking spots.
2. **Supports are forever.** A placed helper never moves again.
3. **One robot, one job.** Each robot fills at most one plan role; the target
   robot always fills the "reach the goal" role.
4. **Blockers dissolve for free.** Route costs ignore robots in the way; the
   vocabulary has no actual "step aside" move. A corollary with teeth: a
   segment that has an exact route on paper is pinned immediately and never
   decomposed — the plan may not say anything else about it.

### Coverage headline

Of the 42 base structural failures: **10** need only Layer 1
(transient cells), **11** need only a park (all of them `blocked_direct`),
**20** have at least one gate outside B1's design (`shared_support` 8,
`target_support` 7, `relocate` 4, `target_clears` 1), and **1** is the
assembly-order residual. So B1 as designed addresses **21/42 (50%)** of the
base ceiling; the other half needs vocabulary or plan-assembly changes B1
deliberately defers (relocation, target roles, shared supports).

At 6 robots the same split holds among the 33 instances the forward planner
could solve and diagnose: **9** Layer-1-only, **11** park-only, **11** with
an uncovered gate (`target_support` 6, `shared_support` 4, `relocate` 1),
**2** residual — i.e. 20/33 (61%) B1-addressable. The other **38**
(32 `NO_COMPLETE_PLAN` + 6 `NO_REALIZABLE_PLAN`, every one a beyond-oracle
instance; all 21 graded structural instances were solved and diagnosed) were
not solved by the forward planner at the standard budget, so no mechanism is
claimed for them; the 6-robot per-family shares should be read as measured on
the diagnosable portion.

Nuance, stated plainly: "not covered" means the *observed solution's* gates
are outside B1's design. B1 might still solve some of these instances through
a different plan shape its new words enable; the B1 stage-3 re-probe is the
measurement that settles it. The converse claim is safe: for these instances
B1's design does not cover the maneuver the real solution used.

## Family mechanics, one paragraph each

**The only sayable plan walks through a robot** (`blocked_direct`). A
walls-only route from the target's start to the goal exists on paper, so the
plan builder pins that walk as the entire plan (rule 4 corollary) and can
never say anything else; in the real game a robot stands on the walk. This
family is **probe-cross-validated at both scales**: it is exactly the set of
`NO_REALIZABLE_PLAN` probe rows that built one complete plan and exhausted
(`complete_tested=1`), and no other instance — solved or unsolved — has that
signature (11 at base, 11 at 6 robots, all graded). The
representative (idx 418, d*=3) is the 3-move puzzle SOURCE_OF_TRUTH §5b cites.
Parks fix precisely this: find the robot whose removal unblocks the failing
slide, move it aside at full cost.

**Park on a wall-less cell** (`transient_support`). A stopper must stand on a
cell no wall can hold; only another robot (placed first) or the plan's timing
makes the cell holdable. Rule 1 filters the pair out before a plan can even
be written. Representative: idx 333, the fully hand-verified worked example
of `analysis/b1_design.md` §2 — its 4-move solution is the gallery's
step-through. Layer 1 exists exactly for this family.

**One parked robot must stop two different sliders** (`shared_support`). One
parking spot does double duty: two movers, at different times, bounce off the
same never-moving robot. Two support roles, one robot — rule 3 refuses the
second recruitment. (A narrow form of sharing is expressible when the second
use rides an already-scheduled support as a route claim; the probe proves
these puzzles cannot arrange it.) Not covered by B1.

**The target robot itself must be the stopper** (`target_support`). A
helper's slide bounces off the target robot before the target finishes. The
target always owns the plan's root role, so it can never be named as a
support — in the starkest base cases the target does not even need to move:
the bounce is off its start cell, and the language still cannot say it (idx
328). Not covered by B1.

**Same robot needed as stopper twice** (`relocate`). A helper serves, moves,
and serves again elsewhere. One leaf, one placement — rule 3 again. At base
scale this gate always co-occurs with others (its instances also need
transient cells and vacates), which is why the B1 design deferred relocation
until the residue demands it; these four instances are that residue's core.

**The target robot must first step out of the way** (`target_clears`). A
helper's delivery route runs through the target's own start cell; the target
steps aside and later returns through where it started. The step-aside has no
plan word (route legs are shortest paths; a detour stop can only be a
supported bottleneck, which this is not), and parks refuse mover robots. The
base representative (idx 109) is a genuine deadlock: the target's only plan
move is its final bounce, yet the helper cannot be delivered while the target
stands at its leaf.

**Stopper must step away after its bounce** (`vacate`) and **a robot in the
way must first clear out** (`idle_clearing`). Both are real gates that at
base scale never end up primary: every observed vacate co-occurs with
relocation (the robot leaves *because* it is needed again), and every
observed idle-clearing instance turned out to be `blocked_direct` (the paper
walk explains it more deeply). Kept in the taxonomy because the gates exist
in combos (and may be primary at other scales); both are what the park
augmentation implements.

**Plan-assembly residual** (`none`). Instances where every individual
maneuver is nameable — all stoppers wall-holdable, nobody vacates or serves
twice, no paper walk — yet the exhaustive probe still proves no expressible
plan plays out. Hand analysis of the base representative (idx 322) locates
the obstruction in plan assembly order: a claimed route may only name a
stopper that is already part of the plan (the declared-stopper invariant,
SOURCE_OF_TRUTH §5a fix 4), and the assembly order this puzzle needs cannot
be reached from the goal outward. B1 does not change assembly order, so its
coverage here is unknown until the re-probe.

## What this buys the project

- The ceiling's composition is now **measured, not guessed**: half the base
  ceiling is B1-shaped (transient cells + parks), half is not (robot-role
  limits: shared/target/relocate roles).
- The **B1 acceptance test gets sharp per-family predictions**: the stage-3
  re-probe should flip `transient_support` and `blocked_direct` instances to
  playable, and should NOT flip `shared_support` / `target_support` /
  `relocate` / `target_clears` ones. A mismatch in either direction is
  information.
- The uncovered half names the next levers precisely: **role relaxation**
  (one robot, several support roles — including the target robot) and
  **assembly-order flexibility**, not more parking vocabulary.
