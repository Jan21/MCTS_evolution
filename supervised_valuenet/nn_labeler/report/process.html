NN Labeler: replacing the exact solver for data labeling across grid sizes

Ricochet Robots planner study · process report · started 2026-07-31 · current through FINDINGS §74 (2026-08-11; live state 2026-08-16) · local document, updated as milestones land. Every number traces to a named file in the repo.

1. The problem

All training data in this study is labeled by exact solvers, and the exact solvers do not scale. Two measured walls (sources: FINDINGS.md §30–34, bench meta sidecars):

  • Backward (subgoal) labels price every candidate at every decision with a full plan-space A* solve. The extended-vocabulary label campaign as first configured projected ~441 node-hours (against ~879 then remaining) — on the Rust engine at 16 threads, i.e. with no faster implementation left. Capping solver iterations rescued the cost (~5 nh) but depleted exactly the candidate type the labels exist to teach (by-reference share 13.5% → 4.8%; the retrain then lost −24.6 points on the frontier). Cost rises along the grid axis: median legitimate-rollout iterations 170–253 (16×16) → 465 (24×24) → 1,062 (32×32).
  • Forward labels and benchmark grading use a move-space A* whose state space is (G²)R. At fixed caps its failure rate is 0% (16×16, 4 robots) → 29.8% (6r) → 40.9% (8r) → 48.4% (24×24/4r) → 64.2% (24×24/8r) → 61.1% (32×32/4r).

The owner's goal: train a neural network that generalizes across grid sizes, then walk up the ladder (17×17, 18×18, … 32×32) generating labeled data with the net. Debug the whole pipeline on small boards (8×8) first. One GPU at a time, small experiments before big ones, ≤16 h walltimes (data-center cooling regime).

2. What made this feasible (research pass, 2026-07-31)

  • The nets were one tensor away from size-invariance. The backward value net is a weight-tied looped transformer over per-cell tokens with slide-graph-masked attention; its only grid-locked weight is a learned absolute positional embedding of shape [G²+1, 192] (train/looped_pc.py:146). Everything else — encoder, the ~741k-parameter looped block, readout — is size-agnostic.
  • Zero-shot transfer already worked once: base-trained (16×16/4r) nets ran unchanged on 6- and 8-robot boards and were slightly better than per-config nets (FINDINGS §29). Retraining on same-size data repeatedly hurt (§34, §47) — evidence favoring a train-once approach.
  • Verifiability: a completed backward plan can be realized to a real move sequence and replayed through pure physics (eval/realize.py::strict_moves, simulate.py). NN-produced labels never need to be trusted — they can be certified playable, and their optimality gap audited wherever the exact solver still works.
  • Board generation is size-generic (RR_GRID env; walls scale as round(48·(G/16)²)); the Rust labeling engine is size-parametric to n≤64.

5. The certified-descent labeler

nn_labeler/descent.py — the engine of Plans B/C. At each decision it enumerates candidates exactly as the exact labeler does (same propose, same top-14 score truncation), but prices each candidate by greedy NN completion instead of an exact solve: commit the candidate, then repeatedly take the value net's best-scored next candidate (keeping the exact-shortest-path fast path, and pruning with the proven prefix_playable check) until the plan completes. The completed plan is realized under full physics; only candidates whose completion actually plays out are labeled. Labels are certified upper bounds in the exact labeler's own plan-cost units; is_optimal = argmin of certified bounds. Records keep the frozen 18-field schema plus provenance (n, label_source, ctg_certified, label_model, label_model_sha) so they are drop-in for the existing trainers and can never be silently confused with exact labels.

done

Plumbing validated at 8×8 with a deliberately weak 1-epoch net: 6/8 RNG instances kept, 11/12 corpus-replay instances kept, ~0.3–0.8 s/instance on CPU. The label-comparison tool (nn_labeler/audit_descent.py) passed an identity self-audit (3040/3040 groups, 20,000/20,000 candidates, all gaps zero) and, against the weak net's labels: zero negative gaps (upper-bound property holds), and on exact-optimal candidates the weak net's completions were exactly optimal 93% of the time (mean gap 0.20) — the structure (exact-pin fast path + candidate enumeration) carries much of the weight even before training. nn_labeler/results (session scratchpad copies); sidecar manifests.

A split verdict against the pre-registered bar (mean ≤ 0.5 and ≤ 10% argmin disagreement): the mean-gap bar is missed at both rungs (0.55–0.57, dragged by a thin tail — max 22/35 — while the median is 0 and p90 ≤ 2); the argmin bar is passed at both (91.5–92.6%). That is the exact inversion of the small rungs, which passed on mean and missed on argmin. Reading: the value head's absolute calibration degrades with board size (matching the growing bias in §6b), while its ordering — the property a labeler actually needs — holds up or improves. And the compute answer, measured rather than argued (FINDINGS §58–59): per kept instance in fresh generation, descent costs 5.1 s (24×24) and 18.6 s (32×32) on one A100 against the Rust exact labeler's 0.030 s / 0.075 s on four CPU cores — 170× / 248×. Even replaying instances already known to be labelable it is still 9× the exact labeler at 32×32. In base vocabulary the method works at scale and saves nothing. Two caveats cutting the same way: the descent labeler is Python for plan manipulation and physics (the network is not the bottleneck), and it yields ~6.9 records per instance against the exact labeler's 8.3.

7c. Where the method actually pays — the payoff experiment

design fixed after an OOM; ran to completion; verdict NEGATIVE (§9b) — FINDINGS §63, §68

§58–59 relocate the claim. The extended (B2) vocabulary is the regime that motivated this whole track: its exact campaign projected ~441 node-hours, and the iteration cap that made it affordable destroyed the labels it existed to produce — the by-reference candidate share fell 13.5% → 4.8%, and the retrain on capped data lost 24.6 points on the frontier. Certified descent has no iteration budget at all — it is a fixed-depth greedy completion, so the wandering-rollout pathology that forced the cap cannot occur and the cost/quality trade-off does not exist. Landing the uncapped by-reference share near 13% rather than the capped 5% would mean the method delivers labels the exact pipeline could not afford to make at all — which is the method's real claim, and not a speed claim.

The first submission died in 91 seconds, and the failure is itself a finding: training on 32×32 boards is the memory wall, not labeling them. The original design trained the B2 net on all five B2 corpora including g32r4; at the base recipe's batch shape (8 groups × ≤32 candidates) a 1,025-token bucket needs ~13 GB of attention scores per step before the recurrence-12 backward graph is even built, and the A100-40GB died at epoch 0, step 3. Every healthy training so far (v1, v2) used ≤257-token boards; inference at 1,025–4,097 tokens has been fine throughout (§7b, §7d) — the backward pass had simply never been exercised above 257 tokens.

The retry is a cleaner experiment than the original. Jobs 4620064→65 (idempotent pair) train on the ≤24 B2 corpora only — g16r4 / g16r6 / g16r8 / g24r8, worst bucket 577 tokens, batch 4 × mpg 16, expandable_segments — and label g32r4 zero-shot. That is the track's own method rather than a workaround, and it makes the g32r4 leg leakage-free where the old design trained on the very corpus it gated against. Training was healthy at ~55 min/epoch with the two-phase curriculum running, and it finished clean (best epoch 25 of 30, no collapse). runs/nnlab/b2_payoff_4618888.out (the OOM traceback); nn_labeler/jobs/b2_payoff.slurm (retry recipe + rationale).

The experiment then returned a negative answer, and the mechanism is worth the paragraph: uncapped NN descent did not restore the by-reference share — it produced almost none. Full accounting in §9b.

7d. The lean-board path — the size wall removed

done — login CPU, no GPU time; FINDINGS §62

§60 measured the real blocker to going past 32×32: it was never the network but nn/gen_grids.make_board, which runs all-pairs Dijkstra and stores two (G²)² tables per board — 222 s and 28.6 MB at 32×32, extrapolating to hours and hundreds of MB at 64. nn_labeler/leanboard.py removes it. The structural fact that makes this safe: those two tables consume no RNG — the random stream is gen_walls → build_graph → random_instance only — so a lean board (walls + graph + instance, no tables) is layout-identical to a standard board by construction, not by approximation. A lazy Dial's-algorithm row oracle with a per-board LRU then answers every distance query the descent call graph makes.

Three levels of evidence, in increasing strength: 1.45 M table entries compared at 16/24 with zero mismatches (including the legacy g16r4 stock boards and their weight-100 all_paths convention); planner candidate lists identical including order and descent-shaped rollouts identical decision-for-decision; and finally an A/B with the real banked net — descent.py --boards pkl vs --boards lean on g16r6 — 69/69 records byte-equal, identical stats. descent.py now takes --boards {pkl,lean} and records the choice in the manifest.

The §58 economics caveat also stands unchanged: in base vocabulary these labels remain cheaper to produce exactly — this rung-by-rung agreement is method validation, not a compute saving.

9. The headline result — label fidelity gates downstream utility

With the ladder answered and the labeler settled, the owner reframed the headline claim (decisions recorded 2026-08-05). The question was no longer "do NN labels agree with exact labels" — §8b answers that at every size — but do planners trained on NN labels perform like planners trained on exact labels. The design:

  • Twin corpora, one variable. The exact corpora at g24r4 / g24r8 / g32r4 were replayed with the banked v1 net: same boards, same instances, same schema — only the label source differs. The study's backward planners were retrained on the twins and benched against the exact-trained originals on the sha-pinned benchmarks (playable-moves scoring, 1200 expansions, k=5, identical hyperparameters). All three cells are leakage-free: the labeler never trained on any of those configs, and every bench reported below is replay-certified.
  • The forward planner stays an untouched control — certified descent keeps no primitive-move sequences, so a forward twin would change the training signal rather than just the label source, and would not answer the question asked.
  • An honest economics correction, made mid-flight: a full-descent twin replay measures 4.3 s/instance — about 12× the depth-0 gate rate the earlier estimates were built on — so the twin corpora became a tens-of-GPU-hours item rather than a few-hour one, and were parallelized per config across lanes instead of chained.

At 91% argmin fidelity the twin planner is indistinguishable from the exact-trained one on everything measured; at 89% it matches solve rate on both the graded and the frontier set (nominally ahead on both) while paying a modest optimality/regret cost; at 82% everything collapses. The campaign self-corrected twice, and both corrections are the point. Its claims were pre-registered to arbiters chosen before the numbers existed, and those arbiters killed two results the narrative would otherwise have kept: v2's clear win on the value audit was rejected by the descent gate (§8, FINDINGS §64), which kept the twin corpora with the stronger labeler; and the first twin row's "beats the exact arm" was demoted to "equivalent" by its seed replicate (FINDINGS §71). 9b. The B2 payoff — a negative result, recorded as one

verdict NEGATIVE — jobs 4620064→4623993, ~32 h GPU (~4 nh); FINDINGS §68

§7c's experiment asked whether uncapped certified descent could restore the by-reference candidate share that the exact pipeline's iteration cap destroyed (13.5% → 4.8%). It could not. The B2 net trained clean (best epoch 25 of 30, no collapse) and then produced by-reference shares of 1.3% at g16r6 and 0.3% at g32r4 — against the 13.5% uncapped-exact target, and below even the 4.8–12.7% capped band. Label quality was correspondingly poor: argmin agreement 53.6% / 50.0%, nowhere near the base-vocabulary curve of §8b.

The mechanism, stated precisely: §7c's structural argument — descent has no iteration budget, so the cap's cost/quality trade-off cannot arise — was correct about the mechanism and wrong about the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the training signal, not the search budget. The only B2 corpora available to train the labeler on were the capped ones, whose by-reference candidates the cap had already destroyed; a net that rarely ranks by-reference candidates first will rarely select them for completion, cap or no cap. Distillation faithfully reproduced the pathology it was meant to cure.

Consequences, taken straight: the compute-saving claim for B2-as-configured is dead — no rescue by distilling capped data is possible. The campaign headline (downstream equivalence in base vocabulary, §9a) is untouched by this, and in hindsight was clearly the right bet. One rescue path stays open and is recorded rather than launched: train the labeler on a small uncapped exact B2 seed corpus — the 13.5% figure proves uncapped exact labeling is affordable at some scale — and see whether the by-reference share survives distillation from clean data. runs/nnlab/b2_payoff_4623993.out (BYREF + audit lines); nn_labeler/results/b2lab_g{16r6,32r4}.jsonl, b2gate_g{16r6,32r4}.json.

What these labels are and are not: every record carries a certified-valid plan — realized to real moves and replayed through pure physics, with uncertifiable completions dropped (25 and 20 respectively) — so they are honest upper bounds. What is unmeasurable, by construction rather than by omission, is the gap to optimal: no solver exists at these sizes to define it. Their only warranty is the flat 17→64 curve, and the files say so in their names: beyond_UNVERIFIABLE_g{80,96}r4.jsonl. Two operational notes worth keeping: the yield thins with size (48% → 28% of sampled instances survive certification, against 52% at 64), and there were zero timeouts at either rung — the network's inference cost is not what limits this direction. Boards at 96×96 took 21 s to make, against the 11.9 h the eager path would have needed (§7d).
