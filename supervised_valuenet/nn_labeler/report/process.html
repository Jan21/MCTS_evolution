NN Labeler: replacing the exact solver for data labeling across grid sizes

Ricochet Robots planner study · process report · started 2026-07-31 · local document, updated as milestones land. Every number traces to a named file in the repo.

1. The problem

All training data in this study is labeled by exact solvers, and the exact solvers do not scale. Two measured walls (sources: FINDINGS.md §30–34, bench meta sidecars):

  • Backward (subgoal) labels price every candidate at every decision with a full plan-space A* solve. The extended-vocabulary label campaign as first configured projected ~441 node-hours (against ~879 then remaining) — on the Rust engine at 16 threads, i.e. with no faster implementation left. Capping solver iterations rescued the cost (~5 nh) but depleted exactly the candidate type the labels exist to teach (by-reference share 13.5% → 4.8%; the retrain then lost −24.6 points on the frontier). Cost rises along the grid axis: median legitimate-rollout iterations 170–253 (16×16) → 465 (24×24) → 1,062 (32×32).
  • Forward labels and benchmark grading use a move-space A* whose state space is (G²)R. At fixed caps its failure rate is 0% (16×16, 4 robots) → 29.8% (6r) → 40.9% (8r) → 48.4% (24×24/4r) → 64.2% (24×24/8r) → 61.1% (32×32/4r).

The owner's goal: train a neural network that generalizes across grid sizes, then walk up the ladder (17×17, 18×18, … 32×32) generating labeled data with the net. Debug the whole pipeline on small boards (8×8) first. One GPU at a time, small experiments before big ones, ≤16 h walltimes (data-center cooling regime).

2. What made this feasible (research pass, 2026-07-31)

  • The nets were one tensor away from size-invariance. The backward value net is a weight-tied looped transformer over per-cell tokens with slide-graph-masked attention; its only grid-locked weight is a learned absolute positional embedding of shape [G²+1, 192] (train/looped_pc.py:146). Everything else — encoder, the ~741k-parameter looped block, readout — is size-agnostic.
  • Zero-shot transfer already worked once: base-trained (16×16/4r) nets ran unchanged on 6- and 8-robot boards and were slightly better than per-config nets (FINDINGS §29). Retraining on same-size data repeatedly hurt (§34, §47) — evidence favoring a train-once approach.
  • Verifiability: a completed backward plan can be realized to a real move sequence and replayed through pure physics (eval/realize.py::strict_moves, simulate.py). NN-produced labels never need to be trusted — they can be certified playable, and their optimality gap audited wherever the exact solver still works.
  • Board generation is size-generic (RR_GRID env; walls scale as round(48·(G/16)²)); the Rust labeling engine is size-parametric to n≤64.

5. The certified-descent labeler

nn_labeler/descent.py — the engine of Plans B/C. At each decision it enumerates candidates exactly as the exact labeler does (same propose, same top-14 score truncation), but prices each candidate by greedy NN completion instead of an exact solve: commit the candidate, then repeatedly take the value net's best-scored next candidate (keeping the exact-shortest-path fast path, and pruning with the proven prefix_playable check) until the plan completes. The completed plan is realized under full physics; only candidates whose completion actually plays out are labeled. Labels are certified upper bounds in the exact labeler's own plan-cost units; is_optimal = argmin of certified bounds. Records keep the frozen 18-field schema plus provenance (n, label_source, ctg_certified, label_model, label_model_sha) so they are drop-in for the existing trainers and can never be silently confused with exact labels.

done

Plumbing validated at 8×8 with a deliberately weak 1-epoch net: 6/8 RNG instances kept, 11/12 corpus-replay instances kept, ~0.3–0.8 s/instance on CPU. The label-comparison tool (nn_labeler/audit_descent.py) passed an identity self-audit (3040/3040 groups, 20,000/20,000 candidates, all gaps zero) and, against the weak net's labels: zero negative gaps (upper-bound property holds), and on exact-optimal candidates the weak net's completions were exactly optimal 93% of the time (mean gap 0.20) — the structure (exact-pin fast path + candidate enumeration) carries much of the weight even before training. nn_labeler/results (session scratchpad copies); sidecar manifests.

6. Debug battery — PE ablation × mixed-size training

battery 1 done — job 4606952, 6h11m (0.77 nh)

8 trainings (20 epochs, seed 11, 96 bins): position-signal ablation {sin2d, coord, none} on 8×8-only and mixed {8,9,10}; single-size references. Each audited at grids 8/9/10/12 and zero-shot 16. Three findings (FINDINGS §50):

  1. The size-free net works, and the best encoding is NO position signal at all. pe=none trained on 8×8 alone: test label regret 0.20 / 0.21 / 0.25 / 0.28 at grids 8/9/10/12 (top-1 optimal 90.5→88.7%) — an essentially flat zero-shot curve to 1.5× the training size — then a collapse at 16×16 (regret 3.39). One-step extrapolation is nearly free; two-step is where zero-shot dies — the first direct evidence on the ladder-shape question (favors the stepwise plan over train-once-jump-everywhere, pending battery 2).
  2. 7 of 8 runs never trained: they sat on a constant-value plateau for all 20 epochs (three different architectures with byte-identical best val_regret 1.9154 — impossible for trained nets, diagnostic for collapse). Root cause: the port dropped the frozen trainer's curriculum warmup; the one healthy arm's 8×8-only data was a natural curriculum. Fixed: curriculum ported (default on), collapse made machine-visible (val_group_spread / pred_group_spread metrics).
  3. Accidental control: a collapsed net's audit measures the hand-written candidate-ordering heuristic — regret 0.72/0.85/0.93/1.18 at 8/9/10/12. The trained net beats it 3.5× in-domain.

nn_labeler/results/audit_d1_*.json, audit_d2_*.json; runs/nnlab/debug_battery_4606952.out; per-run metrics.csv.

battery 2 done — false start 4607697 (0.08 nh) + rerun 4608951 (5h31m, 0.69 nh)

The first battery-2 submission uncovered a second infrastructure failure mode: a curriculum batch-sampler that yields fewer batches than its declared length silently disables Lightning's end-of-epoch validation — no metrics, no checkpoints, no error. Caught by a 7-minute health check; the curriculum was rebuilt as two static fit phases (warmup on the easiest 30% of decision groups, then the full monitored fit) with a post-fit guard that makes silent no-validation impossible (FINDINGS §52). The rerun then settled everything (FINDINGS §53):

  • pe=none is the production encoding — all six no-position-signal arms trained healthily and the seed pairs agree to ±0.03 regret; both sin2d arms stayed collapsed even with the curriculum (the additive sinusoid itself blocks this recipe).
  • Mixed-size training helps everywhere: {8,9,10} reaches test regret 0.16/0.17/0.21/0.24 at grids 8/9/10/12 vs 0.20–0.34 for single-size training.
  • The "16×16 cliff" was a legacy-corpus artifact. On fresh standard-pipeline corpora the same checkpoint reads 0.23/0.30/0.27/0.30 at grids 11/13/14/15 and 0.47 at 16×16-with-6-robots; the legacy corpus's 3.3 measures corpus shift, not board size.

nn_labeler/results/audit_c2_*.json, audit_c2_mix_none_s11_fullcurve.json.

6b. The extrapolation curve — no cliff to 3.2× the training size

done — FINDINGS §54

The decisive measurement: the mixed {8,9,10}-trained pe=none net, audited zero-shot on fresh exact corpora up to 32×32 (using the study's existing 24×24 and 32×32 exact base-vocab corpora as free audit points). A net that never saw a board larger than 10×10 ranks candidates at 32×32 with 81.4% top-1 optimality. The only systematic drift is a growing negative bias at scale (−1.9 at 32×32: long costs are underestimated) — the natural target for the production net's wider training sizes.

8. The ladder — 17×17 to 32×32

path decided — FINDINGS §55

Adopted: Plan C's train-once spine with Plan B's per-rung audit gates; Plan A shelved; the exact solver retired from bulk labeling but retained as the per-rung referee. The ladder deliberately sits in the regime (4 robots, base vocabulary) where the Rust exact solver remains affordable to 32×32, so every rung's NN-generated labels stay verifiable against ground truth — the method's value transfers to the regimes where the exact solver dies (extended vocabularies, 8 robots, beyond 32×32).

production net v1 — trained, collapsed at epoch 2, best epoch banked (FINDINGS §57)

Job 4609800 (~2.0 nh): warmup fit healthy (val_regret 0.734→0.471), full fit epochs 0–1 healthy (0.267 / 0.276, val_group_spread 2.12, top-1 0.89), then constant-value collapse at epoch 2 held for 24 epochs (spread exactly 0.00, top-1 0.12–0.24). This is FINDINGS §44's value-net bistability with a new signature — mid-training rather than cold-start — and the spread metric added after battery 1 diagnosed it from the metrics file alone.
