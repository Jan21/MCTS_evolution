## 1. The question

Two planners solve the same Ricochet Robots puzzles under identical rules:

- **forward** — move-by-move search over primitive robot slides, guided by a
  neural network. Trained by imitating an exact solver.
- **backward** — subgoal search. Builds an abstract plan ("park helper H on
  cell S so the slider stops on B"), then converts it to real moves.

**Claim under test:** subgoal planning is more efficient and more useful as
puzzles scale (more robots, bigger boards).

**The scoring rule that makes it honest:** a puzzle counts as solved only if
the abstract plan **plays out legally, move by move, on the real board**. This
is what took the backward planner from a self-reported 99.6% to 53.1% when it
was first enforced; everything since has been earning that back legitimately.

**Why scale is the crux:** the forward planner learns from an exact solver.
That solver fails on 0% of base puzzles but 40.9% at 8 robots and 61.1% at
32×32 — its teacher dies. The backward planner can learn from its own
successes.

## 2. Methodology

**Configurations ("rungs"):** `g16r4` (16×16, 4 robots — base, benchmark
`eval/data/bench450.jsonl`, boards 2400–2549), `g16r6`, `g16r8`, `g24r4`,
`g24r8`, `g32r4`. Each has 450 pinned benchmark puzzles, 3 per board.

**Puzzle sets.** Each rung's 450 split into *graded* (the exact solver produced
an optimum) and *beyond-oracle / frontier* (it did not). The frontier set is
defined by the oracle's failure, so it is adversarial to the move-level
planner by construction — hence the **pooled** view (graded + frontier = all
450), which carries no selection and is the headline.

**Matched budget.** Every head-to-head: 1,200 search steps, top-5 proposals,
same instances, same machine. One expansion = one popped node whose children
are generated = one policy pass + one batched value pass in both systems.

**Plan-language generations.** old → **B1** (wall-less transient stoppers,
park repairs) → **B2** (a plan may re-use a robot it already placed, plus
generalized step-asides). "Backward" now means B2 unless stated.

**Statistics.** Exact two-sided McNemar on discordant pairs; 95% CIs from a
percentile bootstrap resampling **boards**, not puzzles, because up to three
puzzles share a board's wall layout. 10,000 resamples, seed 0. Pairing is
refused unless both files record the same `protocol.instances_sha256`.
→ `eval/stats_tests.py` → `eval/results/stats_tests.json` (77 cells).

**Independent certification.** Every solved row can emit its realized move
sequence (`--dump-moves`); `eval/replay_validate.py` replays it through the
`simulate.py` physics layer alone — no realizer, no plan classes — and
requires every move to be a real slide and the target to finish on the goal.
**All Track 1 rows to date: zero certification failures.**

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Headline — whole benchmark, no selection to argue about

Backward (full language, zero-shot ranking) vs forward, all 450 puzzles:

| rung | backward | forward | difference (95% CI) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16×16 · 6r | 414/450 = 92.0% | 379/450 = 84.2% | +7.8 [+4.2, +11.6] | <0.0001 |
| 16×16 · 8r | 425/450 = 94.4% | 354/450 = 78.7% | +15.8 [+11.6, +20.0] | <0.0001 |
| 24×24 · 8r | 309/450 = 68.7% | 201/450 = 44.7% | +24.0 [+19.6, +28.7] | <0.0001 |
| 32×32 · 4r | 350/450 = 77.8% | 135/450 = 30.0% | +47.8 [+42.7, +52.7] | <0.0001 |

The margin grows monotonically along both hardness axes. At base scale
(16×16·4r, whose benchmark is entirely gradable) forward wins outright,
450/450 vs 430/450.

### 3.2 With properly retrained networks (g16r6, the one rung with a good corpus)

| set | backward | forward | difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| graded | 310/316 = 98.1% | 314/316 = 99.4% | −1.3 [−3.1, +0.3] | 0.289 |
| beyond-oracle | 105/134 = 78.4% | 65/134 = 48.5% | +29.9 [+20.0, +39.7] | <0.0001 |
| **pooled** | **415/450 = 92.2%** | 379/450 = 84.2% | **+8.0 [+4.7, +11.8]** | <0.0001 |

Forward's gradable-set edge is **no longer statistically distinguishable**.

### 3.3 The training-corpus result (FINDINGS §36) — the campaign's best experiment

Same networks, same recipe, same warm-start, same evaluation. **Only the
training corpus differs.**

| set | cap-20,000 corpus | cap-5,000 corpus | difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| graded | 98.1% | 95.3% | +2.8 [+0.7, +5.1] | 0.023 |
| beyond-oracle | 78.4% | 56.0% | **+22.4 [+14.2, +30.8]** | <0.0001 |
| pooled | 92.2% | 83.6% | +8.7 [+5.6, +12.0] | <0.0001 |

**Why:** label generation caps how long each solving attempt may run. The cheap
cap stripped "by-reference" candidates — the very vocabulary B2 adds — from
13.5% down to 4.6% on identical boards. Networks trained on the depleted
corpus learned to under-rank that candidate type. This is a **data-generation
artifact that looked like a method failure**, and it passed every QC gate the
prior handoff specified.

### 3.4 Budget sensitivity (FINDINGS §28, §31, §35)

Forward given 4–5× the budget on seeded frontier subsamples (backward stays at
1,200):

| rung | fwd @1200 | fwd @4–5× | gain | backward lead after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16×16 · 6r | 40.6% | 62.5% | +21.9 | +9.4, p=0.58 |
| 16×16 · 8r | 43.8% | 75.0% | +31.2 | +18.8, p=0.070 |
| 24×24 · 8r | 15.6% | 28.1% | +12.5 | +18.8, p=0.146 |
| **32×32 · 4r** | 0/24 | **1/24** | **+4.2** | **+45.8, p=0.0010** |

**Consequence:** "beyond the oracle the move-level formulation has stopped
working" is supportable **only at 32×32**. At the other rungs the frontier
margin is a matched-budget result and must be described as such. The
matched-budget comparison — the study's actual protocol — is unaffected.

### 3.5 Budget sweep (`eval/budget_table.py` → `budget_table.json`)

14 budget points per arm per rung/set, reconstructed at zero compute (both
searches are deterministic; the budget only truncates). Two facts invisible at
a single point:

- **Backward saturates by ~100 steps** on gradable sets; forward is still
  climbing at 1,200. Reporting only 1,200 understates the efficiency gap.
- **The crossover moves with scale:** forward catches up by ~400 steps at
  16×16 and 24×24, and **never** at 32×32 (57.7% vs 88.0% at 400).

### 3.6 The language story (FINDINGS §37) — reframe needed

B1 and B2 rows at base and g16r6 use **identical networks**, making this a
clean language test:

| set | old | B1 | B2 zero-shot | old→B1 | B1→B2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base 450 | 401 | 429 | 430 | **+28** | +1 |
| g16r6 graded | 275 | 304 | 306 | **+29** | +2 |
| g16r6 frontier | 70 | 108 | 108 | **+38** | **0** |

**Essentially the whole executable-language gain is B1.** B2 lifts the
*ceiling* (97.6% → 99.6% at base — what a plan can express) but the trained
planner does not realise it, even on a good corpus. The paper should present
B1 as carrying the gain and B2 as a ceiling result plus evidence that the
residual gap is **ranking, not expressibility**.

### 3.7 Other measured results

- **Selection is not the explanation.** 44 oracle-independent hardness strata
  (goal wall-backing × target travel): backward leads **43, ties 1, loses
  none**; 39 significant. → `analysis/frontier_strata.py`
- **No train/test leakage.** Wall-layout Jaccard across train/val/bench, all
  six configs: 0 exact duplicates, 0 pairs ≥ 0.9, max 0.185.
  → `analysis/dedup_audit.py`
- **Supervision is lopsided against backward:** forward trains on 10.6–18.5×
  more records from the same boards. → `analysis/data_budget.py`
- **Matched physics unit.** `simulate.slide` counted in both stacks. Physics
  only, same 5 puzzles: backward 64 vs forward 5,776 median (~90×), against
  ~99× on expansions — the efficiency claim survives a matched unit
  essentially unchanged. NB ~86% of forward's raw slide count is neural-network
  featurization, not search; quoting the raw total (40,448) overstates it ~7×.
- **Tuning ledger** (`analysis/tuning_ledger.md`): neither side got a
  learning-rate sweep; both are single-seed. The base forward cell is the
  **best of four** independently trained systems against a single backward run.

## 6. Operating rules earned this session (each cost real time)

- **Never size a generation job from its opening graphs.** The first
  projection was wrong by 12–42×; the label campaign was heading for 441
  node-hours and would have produced nothing. Smoke-test with `--limit` and
  project from the steady-state rate. `jobs/patterns/b2_labels_smoke.slurm`.
- **A completion marker must be gated on the thing it claims.** Three
  incidents in one campaign (FINDINGS §38): a retrain that printed `DONE` over
  a crash and left a config with no value net for 15 h; two lanes that
  measured nothing and reported success; a certification that could print
  `DONE` over its own failure.
- **A QC gate should assert a distribution, not a range.** The deficient
  corpus passed every specified gate and sat at the bottom of the expected
  band — at one rung below it, without firing.
- **Never `kill`/`pkill` by pattern** — it matched this session's own shell and
  killed a watcher, stalling the pipeline for five hours. Kill by PID.
- **Cross-validation-split metrics are not comparable.** A banking criterion
  comparing `val_regret` across differently-derived validation sets was
  pre-registered, then retracted (FINDINGS §33b) — it flagged healthy runs as
  failures.
- Verify a watcher is alive after arming it. Two were silently dead.
